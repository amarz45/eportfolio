I chose computer science because it keeps my options open. I didn't know what I
wanted to specialise in, and the field is general enough that I could commit to
it without committing to one narrow path. It also happens to be the thing I do
in my own time, so most of the material comes easily to me.

In the near term I want to keep doing work that removes tedious problems for
other people, which is most of what my co-op term has been. Longer term I'm
drawn to software that people actually switch to – tools good enough that the
cost of leaving whatever they were using stops being the deciding factor.

## Where I want this to go

Concretely, over the next two years:

- **Ship Termtile to a working v1** that students other than me can actually
  use, by the end of 2026.
- **Get deep in one systems area** – parallelism, performance, or security
  tooling – far enough to be the person a team asks about it, rather than a
  generalist everywhere. Comicpress is where this started: the parallelism
  problem was the part I enjoyed most.
- **Publish a project built with a technology I haven't used before**, with a
  README good enough that a stranger can run it, by March 2027.
- **Land a software engineering internship for summer 2027**, ideally somewhere
  the work is closer to systems than to applications.

The measurable versions of these, with deadlines, are on the
[Interests and Skills]({{< relref "/goals/interests-and-skills" >}}) page.
