A reflective journal kept across my co-op term for COOP 2100, tracking what I
worked on each week and what it taught me. The journal itself contains workplace
detail I've kept out of a public site; this page is a reflection on it rather
than the document.

## What it recorded

I logged progress every two business days from the start of the term, which gave
me an unusually complete record to write from: the first weeks learning
platforms I'd never touched, the first automation I shipped, the failures in
between, and the shift from automating monthly reporting to automating
day-to-day work.

## What I actually learned from writing it

Two things surfaced that I would not have noticed without the record.

The first is that my supervisor asking "what is the objective, why are you doing
it, and how will it help the team?" changed how I communicate about my work.
Reading my updates in order, the early ones lead with mechanics and the later
ones lead with impact. That's a measurable change in behaviour, and I only saw
it because I had eight weeks of my own writing to compare.

The second is about what is actually worth automating. My early work targeted
monthly reporting, which is the obvious candidate: repetitive, well-defined, and
easy to point at. But reading the term back in order, most of the time on my
team goes to day-to-day tasks, which are harder to automate, more error-prone,
and carry more upfront cost – which is exactly why they tend to get skipped. The
second half of my term shifted toward those, and it changed how I pick problems.
The easiest thing to automate is rarely the thing most worth automating.

## What I'd do differently

I let my learning objectives emerge over the first few weeks rather than setting
them deliberately on day one. It worked out, but the first fortnight had less
direction than it needed to, and I'd define objectives explicitly at the start of
a future placement.

## Competencies demonstrated

**Communication Skills** — a sustained update cadence with a supervisor, and the
shift from describing mechanics to describing impact. **Individual and
Teamwork** — the work documented here was largely independent, but its value was
measured by whether teammates could use what I built without me. **Professionalism**
— the journal is where I had to be honest about what hadn't gone well.
**Life-Long Learning** — reflective practice as a way of noticing change that is
invisible day to day.
