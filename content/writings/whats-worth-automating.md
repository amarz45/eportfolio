---
title: "What’s Actually Worth Automating"
summary: "The easiest thing to automate is rarely the thing most worth automating."
---

I logged my progress every two business days across my first co-op term, which
left me with an unusually complete record to read back: the first weeks learning
platforms I'd never touched, the first automation I shipped, the failures in
between, and a shift partway through in the kind of work I was targeting. Two
things surfaced that I would not have noticed without the record.

## What's worth automating isn't what's easiest to automate

My early work targeted monthly reporting, which is the obvious candidate:
repetitive, well-defined, and easy to point at. But reading the term back in
order, most of the time on my team goes to day-to-day tasks. Those are harder to
automate, more error-prone, and carry more upfront cost, which is exactly why
they tend to get skipped, and exactly why the payoff is larger when they don't.
The second half of my term shifted toward those, and it changed how I pick
problems.

## Leading with impact instead of mechanics

My supervisor's standing question “what is the objective, why are you doing it,
and how will it help the team?” changed how I write about my own work. Reading
my updates in order, the early ones lead with mechanics and the later ones lead
with impact. That's a measurable change in behaviour, and I only saw it because
I had eight weeks of my own writing to compare.

## What I'd do differently

I let my objectives emerge over the first few weeks rather than setting them
deliberately on day one. It worked out, but the first two weeks had less
direction than it needed, and I'd define them explicitly at the start of a
future placement.
